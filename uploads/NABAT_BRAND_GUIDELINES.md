# 01 — Brand Foundation

*Science · Smart · Stable · Nature · Business · Pragmatic · Tech*

---

Nabat is not a conservation platform. It is not a data dashboard. It is not a startup built around an environmental trend.

**Nabat is an operating system for nature.**

That distinction is the single most important idea in this document. Everything — visual, verbal, structural — flows from it. An operating system does not emote. It does not campaign. It provides reliable infrastructure that allows other things to function at scale. It is **precise**, **quiet**, and **powerful**.

The visual identity of Nabat reflects this positioning. It is restrained where others are loud. It is editorial where others are promotional. It is technical where others are illustrative. It is built to earn the trust of scientists, ecologists, enterprise partners, and government agencies — not to inspire a general audience.

---

### BRAND POSITIONING STATEMENT

```
Nabat is the operating system for nature —
a platform that bridges ecological intelligence
with enterprise-grade infrastructure,
enabling organizations to understand, report,
and act on biodiversity at scale.
```

# 02 — Mission & Vision

---

## Mission

> *To make nature legible to the modern world — with the precision of science, the reliability of infrastructure, and the ambition of a platform company.*

Nabat exists to close the gap between ecological reality and organizational decision-making. Biodiversity is collapsing at a rate that outpaces the tools available to measure, report, or respond to it. Nabat changes that.

---

## Vision

> *A world where every organization that touches nature — by extracting from it, restoring it, financing it, or governing it — has access to the same quality of ecological intelligence that was previously available only to the world's leading research institutions.*

This is not a distant aspiration. It is the product roadmap.

---

## The Problem We Are Solving

### **The Intelligence Gap**

Organizations that depend on or impact nature — mining companies, banks, governments, conservation bodies, agriculture firms — are making decisions without adequate ecological data. Not because the data doesn't exist. Because no one has built the infrastructure to make it usable.

**Nabat is that infrastructure.**

### **The Reporting Crisis**

Regulatory frameworks (TNFD, CSRD, EU Taxonomy, biodiversity net gain legislation) now require organizations to disclose their nature-related risks and impacts. Most organizations cannot comply. They lack the data, the methodology, and the tooling.

**Nabat is the compliance layer.**

### **The Restoration Gap**

Billions of dollars are flowing into nature-based solutions — carbon credits, biodiversity credits, restoration projects — without adequate monitoring or verification infrastructure. Fraud, miscounting, and greenwashing are endemic.

**Nabat is the verification layer.**

# 03 — Brand Voice & Tone

---

## Voice Characteristics

The Nabat voice is **authoritative without being arrogant**. It is **technical without being cold**. It is **urgent without being alarmist**. It speaks the language of science and the language of business with equal fluency.

---

### VOICE PILLARS

**1 · Precise**
We do not approximate when we can be exact. We say "87 species recorded across 4,200 hectares" not "rich biodiversity across a wide area." Precision is not pedantry — it is trust.

**2 · Restrained**
We do not oversell. We do not use superlatives we cannot defend. We do not claim to "save the planet." We build tools. We provide data. We enable decisions.

**3 · Editorial**
Our communications have weight and structure. We think in sections, arguments, and evidence. We write for people who read carefully.

**4 · Grounded**
Nature is the subject. We treat it with scientific respect, not romantic projection. We describe ecosystems the way ecologists describe them — with specificity, rigor, and appropriate uncertainty.

**5 · Forward**
We are building for what comes next. The ecological crises of our moment are not abstract future concerns — they are present operational realities. Our voice reflects urgency without hysteria.

---

## Tone Modulation

*The voice stays constant. The tone shifts with context.*

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Scientific / Technical | Neutral, methodological | "Acoustic monitoring data was processed using a validated species classifier with 94.3% accuracy across target taxa." |
| Executive / Business | Confident, outcomes-focused | "Nabat reduces biodiversity reporting cycles from 18 months to 6 weeks." |
| Regulatory / Compliance | Precise, thorough | "All assessments conform to TNFD LEAP methodology and IUCN Red List threat classifications." |
| Editorial / Thought Leadership | Reflective, ambitious | *"We are at an inflection point in our relationship with the natural world — not as a species, but as an economy."* |
| UI / Product | Clear, functional, minimal | "3 monitoring stations active · Last synced 4 min ago" |

---

## What We Say vs. What We Don't

| We say | We don't say |
|--------|-------------|
| "Biodiversity intelligence" | "Eco-tech" |
| "Nature-related risk and impact" | "Saving the planet" |
| "Ecological infrastructure" | "Green solution" |
| "Verified biodiversity data" | "Nature scores" |
| "Enterprise-grade monitoring" | "Easy, intuitive platform" |
| "Systematic species assessment" | "AI-powered nature AI" |
| "Monitoring infrastructure" | "Revolutionary technology" |

---

## Writing Style Rules

- **Short sentences.** Long ideas. These are not the same thing.
- **Active voice.** Always. "Nabat monitors" not "monitoring is provided by Nabat."
- **No hedging.** Avoid "may," "might," "could potentially" unless scientifically necessary.
- **Lead with fact.** Open with the most important information. Never bury the lede.
- **No jargon for its own sake.** Technical terms are earned, not decorative.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "4,200 species" beats "thousands of species."
- *Italics for editorial voice.* Reserve them for pull quotes, key phrases, and emphasis.
- **Capitals for emphasis only.** Not for decoration. Not for "important" words.

# 04 — Brand Principles

---

## *Seven principles that govern every decision.*

---

**Principle 01 · Infrastructure Over Inspiration**

Nabat is infrastructure. Infrastructure is not inspiring — it is reliable, scalable, and invisible when it works. Our design language reflects this. We do not use heroic imagery. We do not use dramatic color. We build systems that work.

---

**Principle 02 · Data as the Primary Medium**

Every design decision must ask: "Does this communicate data clearly?" Typography, color, spacing — all serve the legibility of information. Decoration is permitted only when it enhances rather than competes with content.

---

**Principle 03 · Restraint as Intelligence**

Less says more when every element earns its presence. A single gold rule on a paper background communicates more sophistication than a complex gradient. Restraint is not limitation — it is editorial intelligence.

---

**Principle 04 · Nature Without Romanticism**

We draw from nature — its palettes, its textures, its rhythms — but we never sentimentalize it. Nature is data. Nature is complexity. Nature is an operating environment. We treat it accordingly.

---

**Principle 05 · Scale Without Sacrifice**

The brand must work at 8px and at 8 meters. A product icon and a conference installation. A PDF report and a live data dashboard. Every element in this system is designed to scale without losing its essential character.

---

**Principle 06 · Light and Dark as Equal Registers**

Nabat operates in two modes with equal confidence. The light mode — warm paper and forest ink — communicates editorial authority and permanence. The dark mode — deep forest and gold — communicates technical depth and precision. Neither is primary.

---

**Principle 07 · Time as a Design Material**

Ecological systems operate on timescales that dwarf human experience. Nabat's design acknowledges this — through stillness, through weight, through a deliberate rejection of the frantic pace of consumer software. Slow is a feature.

## Color Usage Rules

**Do:**
- Use `--brand-primary` (`#246C5F`) for primary interactive elements and identity moments
- Use `--gold` and `--gold-bright` for decorative rules, borders, and editorial emphasis
- Use warm paper tones for print and report backgrounds
- Use dark forest tones for dashboards, data-dense interfaces, and premium contexts
- Maintain WCAG AA contrast (minimum 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text)

**Do not:**
- Use brand green as a background color in large quantities
- Mix warm paper tones with dark forest tones (they are separate registers, not gradients)
- Use pure black (`#000000`) or pure white (`#FFFFFF`) — always use the ink/paper system
- Apply gold as a primary call-to-action color — it is decorative, not functional

# 06 — Typography

---

## Font Families

### Cormorant Garamond — *The Editorial Voice*

*Weights: 300 · 400 · 500 · 600 · 700 · Italic available*

Cormorant Garamond is the primary display typeface. It carries editorial authority, historical weight, and the kind of quiet elegance that earns attention without demanding it. Its high contrast — thin hairlines against broad strokes — mirrors the tension between data precision and ecological scale.

**Use for:** Headlines, display text, manifesto statements, pull quotes, section openers, the masthead, drop caps, numbered items in ledger layouts, stat figures.

**Do not use for:** Body text at sizes below 18px, UI elements, buttons, form labels, data tables.

---

### Inter Tight — *The Operational Voice*

*Weights: 300 · 400 · 500 · 600*

Inter Tight is the system typeface. Tightly spaced and geometrically precise, it communicates clarity, efficiency, and technical credibility. It is the voice of the platform — the UI, the documentation, the operational layer.

**Use for:** Body text, UI labels, buttons, captions, form elements, metadata, navigation, all text below 16px that requires consistent legibility.

**Do not use for:** Large display headings, creative editorial content, or anywhere Cormorant Garamond would be more appropriate.

---

### JetBrains Mono — *The Technical Voice*

*Weights: 300 · 400 · 500*

JetBrains Mono is the precision typeface. Used exclusively for code, technical labels, API references, data values, and any fixed-width content. Its monospace rhythm communicates exactness.

**Use for:** Code blocks, API endpoints, data field labels, technical taxonomy references, species identifiers, sensor IDs, version strings.

**Do not use for:** General UI text, headings, or any context where a proportional typeface is more appropriate.

## Typography — Accessibility Notes

- **Minimum body size:** 13px for reading content; 9px only for non-critical labels
- **Minimum line height:** 1.45 (editorial); 1.7 (web body text)
- **Maximum line length:** 560–640px (60–75 characters)
- **Italic usage:** Cormorant Garamond italic is reserved for emphasis and editorial voice — never for body text
- **Weight contrast:** Use weight difference (300 vs 600) to establish hierarchy without relying on color alone

The OS badge is a visual shorthand for the platform positioning. It appears as a compact typographic mark adjacent to the wordmark, set in a condensed all-caps style. It is not decorative — it is definitional.

**Rules:**
- Never separate the OS badge from the logotype in primary contexts
- The OS badge is always set in a lighter weight than the wordmark
- In very small contexts (below 80px width), the OS badge may be omitted

## Logo Misuse — What Never to Do

- Do not rotate or skew the logo
- Do not apply drop shadows or effects
- Do not change the typeface
- Do not use unapproved color combinations
- Do not place the logo on backgrounds that create insufficient contrast
- Do not stretch or distort proportions
- Do not recreate the logo from scratch — always use the provided vector files
- Do not use the logo in decorative or illustrative ways

# 11 — Dark Mode System

## Philosophy

The Nabat dark mode is not an inversion of the light mode. It is a separate register — the same brand, in a different environment. Where light mode communicates permanence and editorial authority, dark mode communicates technical depth, precision, and nocturnal focus.

*Think: the inside of a field station at 2am. Scientific instruments glowing in the dark. Data streams updating. The forest is outside; you are processing what it has told you.*

# 13 — Motion & Animation

---

## Motion Philosophy

Nabat's motion design is **calibrated, not choreographed**. Transitions communicate state changes, not personality. They are fast enough to feel responsive, slow enough to feel considered.

**The question for every animation:** *Does this motion carry meaning, or does it carry attention?* If the answer is the latter, remove it.

---

## Timing Reference

| Name | Duration | Easing | Usage |
|------|----------|--------|-------|
| **Instant** | 0ms | — | Immediate state changes |
| **Quick** | 80ms | ease-out | Hover states, micro-interactions |
| **Standard** | 150ms | ease-in-out | Component transitions |
| **Deliberate** | 250ms | ease-in-out | Panel slides, modal opens |
| **Slow** | 400ms | ease-in | Page transitions, complex reveals |
| **Ecological** | 600–1200ms | cubic-bezier | Wave animations, data emergence |

---

## Easing Functions

```css
:root {
  --ease-standard: cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.0, 0.2, 1);
  --ease-enter:    cubic-bezier(0.0, 0.0, 0.2, 1);
  --ease-exit:     cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.0, 1.0, 1);
  --ease-sharp:    cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.0, 0.6, 1);
  --ease-natural:  cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.46, 0.45, 0.94);
}
```

Nabat icons are **technical, not illustrative**. They communicate function, not concept. They borrow from the vocabulary of scientific instruments, field equipment, and data visualization rather than from consumer app conventions.

---

## Icon Grid & Sizing

| Size | Grid | Stroke | Usage |
|------|------|--------|-------|
| 16px | 16×16 | 1.5px | Dense UI, table cells |
| 20px | 20×20 | 1.5px | Inline UI icons |
| 24px | 24×24 | 1.5px | Standard UI icons |
| 32px | 32×32 | 2px | Feature icons, empty states |
| 48px | 48×48 | 2px | Illustration icons |
| 60px | 60×60 | 2px | Large decorative icons |

---

## Icon Style

- **Stroke, not fill** — All icons use outlined paths; no filled icons
- **Consistent stroke weight** — 1.5px at 24px grid; 2px at 32px+
- **Rounded terminations** — `stroke-linecap: round; stroke-linejoin: round`
- **Minimal detail** — Reduce to essential form; no decorative detail
- **No gradient strokes** — Icons are always flat color

# 16 — Accessibility

---

## Contrast Standards

All text combinations must meet WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Critical interfaces should target AAA.

| Combination | Ratio | Standard |
|-------------|-------|----------|
| `#0A1410` on `#FAFAF5` | 18.4:1 | AAA |
| `#0A1410` on `#F4EFE4` | 16.8:1 | AAA |
| `#246C5F` on `#F4EFE4` | 5.2:1 | AA |
| `#F4EFE4` on `#0B1F17` | 16.2:1 | AAA |
| `#D4B577` on `#0B1F17` | 7.8:1 | AAA |
| `#63D487` on `#0B1F17` | 9.4:1 | AAA |
| `#246C5F` on `#FFFFFF` | 5.4:1 | AA |
| `#B89968` on `#0A1410` | 4.9:1 | AA |

---

## Typography Accessibility

- **Minimum body size:** 13px (Inter Tight) or 16px (Cormorant Garamond) for reading
- **Minimum label size:** 9px with adequate letter-spacing (0.18em+) and uppercase
- **Line height minimum:** 1.45 for all reading content
- **Maximum line length:** 640px (approximately 75 characters)
- **Paragraph spacing:** Minimum 14px between paragraphs

---

## Focus States

All interactive elements require a visible focus indicator:

```css
:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid #246C5F;
  outline-offset: 2px;
}

/* On dark backgrounds */
[data-theme="dark"] :focus-visible {
  outline-color: #63D487;
}
```

---

## Motion & Cognitive Accessibility

- All decorative animations are pausable or disabled via `prefers-reduced-motion`
- Time limits on data refresh are configurable
- No content flashes faster than 3 times per second (WCAG 2.3.1)
- Status changes communicated via ARIA live regions, not color alone

---

## Screen Reader Support

- All icons have `aria-hidden="true"` with visible label or `aria-label`
- All images have descriptive `alt` text or `alt=""` if purely decorative
- Color is never the sole means of conveying information
- Table headers use proper `<th scope>` attributes
- Form fields have explicit `<label>` associations

# 18 — Do's & Don'ts

---

## Color

| ✓ Do | ✗ Don't |
|------|---------|
| Use `#246C5F` for primary interactive elements | Use brand green as a large background color |
| Use gold (`#B89968`) for borders and editorial rules | Use gold as a button or CTA color |
| Use warm paper tones on light mode surfaces | Mix paper tones with cool whites |
| Use forest tones for dark mode exclusively | Use dark forest green for text on light backgrounds |
| Apply `#D4B577` (gold-bright) on dark backgrounds | Apply `#B89968` (gold) on dark — insufficient contrast |
| Use `#0A1410` (ink) for all body text on light | Use pure black `#000000` anywhere in the system |

---

## Typography

| ✓ Do | ✗ Don't |
|------|---------|
| Use Cormorant Garamond for display and editorial | Use Cormorant Garamond for body text below 18px |
| Use Inter Tight for all UI and functional text | Use Cormorant Garamond for UI labels, buttons, or forms |
| Use JetBrains Mono for code and technical data | Use JetBrains Mono for decorative or general text |
| Set body line-height at minimum 1.45 | Set line-height below 1.2 for any reading text |
| Use italic Cormorant for pull quotes and editorial | Use italic for body text emphasis |
| Keep body text max-width at 560–640px | Allow text to run full-width across large screens |

---

## Logo

| ✓ Do | ✗ Don't |
|------|---------|
| Use the provided vector files | Recreate the logo from scratch |
| Maintain clear space on all sides | Place other elements within the clear zone |
| Use in approved color combinations only | Apply the logo in unapproved colors or on low-contrast backgrounds |
| Scale proportionally | Stretch, compress, or distort in any dimension |
| Use the OS badge in primary contexts | Remove the OS badge without reason |

---

## Tone of Voice

| ✓ Do | ✗ Don't |
|------|---------|
| Lead with specifics: "87 species, 4,200 ha" | Write vague generalities: "rich biodiversity" |
| Write in active voice | Use passive constructions: "data is collected by" |
| State facts confidently without hedging | Use excessive qualifiers: "may," "might," "potentially" |
| Use the word "infrastructure" | Use the phrase "game-changer" |
| Reference ecological science accurately | Simplify or romanticize ecology |
| Describe what Nabat does | Claim Nabat will "save the planet" |
