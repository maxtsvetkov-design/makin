## Activation criteria

**Content types:**
- ✅ Articles and blog posts
- ✅ Documentation and guides
- ✅ Press releases and announcements
- ✅ Interface copy and long-form UX text
- ✅ Marketing materials and campaigns
- ✅ Any content where AI detection would damage credibility

**Use cases:**
- Creating website copy and product descriptions
- Drafting sustainability reports or impact statements
- Writing social media content about environmental initiatives
- Reviewing any AI-assisted content before publication
- Preparing grant applications or partnership proposals
- Writing pitch decks or investor communications


## Pre-output checklist

Before returning output, verify:

**Universal:**
- No sentence appears twice
- Every financial claim includes specific numbers
- Technology descriptions explain what the tech actually does
- No buzzwords from the banned list

**Quick search:**
1. "delve" → delete or replace
2. "landscape" → delete or replace
3. "crucial" → is it actually crucial?
4. "robust" → what does this mean here?
5. "leverage" → just say "use"
6. "comprehensive" → is this word doing any work?
7. Starting "So," → delete


## Output templates

### Case study structure

- Challenge: [Specific technical/ecological problem]
- Solution: [Exact technology/methodology used]
- Outcome: [Quantified environmental result]

Example:

Challenge: Manual forest monitoring is costly, low-resolution, and slow to act on
Solution: High-resolution imagery and proprietary AI models for automated forest monitoring and biodiversity assessment
Outcome: Delivered automated species detection & count, and data-driven ecosystem management decisions with cost saving

### Data presentation rules

**Always include:**
- Specific measurements: "4.53 ha," "26.72510 • 49.98781"
- Percentage changes: "+8% QoQ," "-22% QoQ"
- Time stamps: "14 May 2025 14:32," "Dec 1, 2025"
- Coverage areas: "524 ha," "7.67 ha"

**Format standards:**
- Coordinates: "26.72510 • 49.98781"
- Areas: "4.53 ha" (space before unit)
- Percentages: "+8%" (include sign)
- Dates: "14 May 2025" (day month year)


## Universal rules

| --- | --- | --- |
| delve | AI signature word | explore, examine, study |
| realm | Pretentious | field, area, space |
| tapestry | Purple prose | (delete entirely) |
| landscape | Overused in sustainability | environment, sector, market |
| leverage | Corporate jargon | use, harness |
| utilize | Unnecessary | use |
| robust | Meaningless modifier | strong, resilient, proven |
| seamless | Almost always false | smooth, integrated |
| comprehensive | Rarely adds value | complete, full |
| cutting-edge | Startup cliché | advanced, new |
| holistic | Overused in sustainability | complete, integrated |

### Banned phrases

**Throat-clearing (delete entirely):**
- "We're excited to announce..."
- "In today's climate crisis..."
- "Let's dive into our solution..."
- "Without further ado..."
- "In this deck, we will show..."
- "It's worth noting that..."
- "As we all know, the planet..."
- "The future of sustainability..."

**Empty hedges:**
- "To be fair..." / "To be honest..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "In terms of..."
- "With respect to..."

**AI enthusiasm:**
- "This is a game-changer"
- "...and that's a good thing!"
- "Here's the thing:"
- "...and that's okay!"
- "...and I'm here for it"

**Corporate buzzwords:**
- "Moving forward..."
- "Going forward..."
- "Compounds"

**Empty transitions:**
- ❌ "With that in mind..."
- ❌ "Building on this foundation..."
- ❌ "Taking this a step further..."
- ✅ Just make the next point

### Banned rhythm patterns (AI staccato)

The most recognizable AI writing pattern is **choppy dramatic fragments** — short sentence followed by a contradicting fragment, used for rhetorical punch. Humans don't write this way. It reads as artificial and manipulative.

**The contrast fragment:**
- ❌ "Forests are disappearing. Technology can help. We built that technology."
- ❌ "Carbon emissions rise. Solutions exist. We found one."
- ❌ "The planet is warming. Time is running out. We're acting now."
- ✅ "While forests disappear at record rates, our technology helps landowners restore 10x faster."
- ✅ "We built carbon capture technology that works at the scale climate change demands."

**The punch-line fragment:**
- ❌ "We studied soil for five years. To save agriculture."
- ❌ "Two problems. One solution. Better farming."
- ✅ "We spent five years studying soil to develop farming methods that restore rather than deplete."
- ✅ "Our technology solves both water scarcity and soil degradation in one system."

**The staccato list:**
- ❌ "No waste. No emissions. No compromise. No excuses."
- ✅ "Our process eliminates waste, reduces emissions by 80%, and maintains product quality."

**Why this matters:** This pattern screams "AI-generated sustainability content." Investors and customers recognize it instantly. It feels like greenwashing, not genuine environmental leadership. Real founders write with natural conviction about problems they've studied deeply.

### Banned structures

**Don't start sentences with:**
- "So," or "Well,"
- "Now," (when not about time)
- "Look," or "Listen,"
- "Basically," or "Essentially,"

**Don't end sentences with:**
- "...right?" (sounds insecure about climate facts)
- "...you know?" (assumes shared knowledge)
- "...if you will" (sounds pretentious)

### Case sensitivity

Always use sentence case for headings, not title case.

| Wrong | Right |
|-------|-------|
| Getting Started With Your Project | Getting started with your project |
| How To Use The Tool | How to use the tool |
| Best Practices For Journalism | Best practices for journalism |


## Domain rules

### Sustainability / nature startups

**Pre-output checklist (in addition to universal):**
- Market size includes [company name]'s target share (ask user for company name if not provided)
- Customer traction includes contract values or hectares
- Team description includes specific expertise numbers
- Problem statements cite specific environmental data
- Solutions include measurable outcomes (% improvement, cost reduction, etc.)
- Every technology claim includes specific capabilities
- Every environmental claim includes quantified metrics
- Every AI reference specifies what the AI actually does
- Every ecosystem reference uses scientific terminology
- Every result includes measurable outcomes
- No generic startup buzzwords appear
- All data includes units and precision levels
- Process descriptions explain the scientific methodology
- Geographic scope is quantified (hectares, countries, sites)
- Time references are specific (dates, growth periods, monitoring frequency)

**Financial claims:**
- ❌ Never write: "$430B+ combined market"
- ✅ Always write: "$430B restoration market, targeting 2% share ($8.6B) by 2030"

**Technology descriptions:**
- ❌ Never write: "proprietary geospatial AI foundation model"
- ✅ Always write: "AI that analyzes satellite data to identify degraded land and predict restoration success rates"

**Market positioning:**
- ❌ Never write: "most critical natural assets"
- ✅ Always write: "mangrove forests (80% degraded), coral reefs (50% bleached), wetlands (70% lost since 1900)"

**Team descriptions:**
- ❌ Never write: "built to operate at the intersection of AI, ecology, and large-scale delivery"
- ✅ Always write: "12 engineers, 8 ecologists, 4 project managers with 200+ restoration projects completed"

**Customer traction:**
- ❌ Never write: "active engagements"
- ✅ Always write: "signed contracts worth $X, pilot projects covering Y hectares, Z qualified opportunities"

### Journalism

**"Not just X—it's Y" pattern:**
- ❌ "This isn't just a news story—it's a wake-up call"
- ❌ "This wasn't just a press conference—it was a turning point"
- ✅ State the thing directly without dramatic framing

**"Fundamentally transforms" pattern:**
- ❌ "This fundamentally transforms how newsrooms operate"
- ❌ "This represents a fundamental shift in journalism"
- ✅ Describe the actual change without hyperbole

**Inflated claims in reporting:**
- ❌ "A bombshell revelation"
- ❌ "A major milestone"
- ❌ "A groundbreaking investigation"
- ✅ Let the facts speak. Readers judge significance.


## Validation

### Red flags in technical/analysis writing

1. **Lists of near-synonyms**: "comprehensive, sophisticated, and robust" (pick one or none)
2. **Excessive hedging**: "may potentially be able to possibly..."
3. **Noun stacking**: "production-ready deployment system infrastructure"
4. **Passive voice hiding agency**: "It was determined that..." (by whom?)
5. **Circular definitions**: "The system enables users to use the functionality"

### The verbal tic test

Read your text aloud. Does it sound like:
- A TED talk introduction? **Rewrite.**
- A LinkedIn post? **Rewrite.**
- A press release? **Rewrite.**
- Corporate communications? **Rewrite.**
- How you'd explain it to a colleague? **Keep it.**

### The ultimate test

Before accepting any AI-assisted text, ask:

1. Can I delete this word/phrase without losing meaning? → Delete it
2. Is this the simplest way to say this? → Simplify
3. Would I say this out loud to a colleague? → If not, rewrite
4. Does this add information or just sound impressive? → If the latter, cut it

**Remember:** If readers think "AI wrote this," you've already lost their trust.
